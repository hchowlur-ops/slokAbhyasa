Placement by sound alone, in order

Written after the recital of 06:34 (CH18-03..06, its take note), where the ear ran 2.8 and 5.6 s late and the second opinion 8 and 12 s late, and the rule of the day before handed the second opinion the window. The question: would the sloka's own sound, placed in order, have got the cuts right — not on this take alone, but on every recital kept since 20 October?

The method

Each sloka's reference recording is located in the whole take by sound (js/dsp/locate.js, the search the comparison already runs when a take is much longer than a sloka, at tempos 0.8–1.25, the best contrast kept). The surest sloka — the lowest contrast — is fixed first. Every other sloka is then searched again only in the room its order leaves it: after the end of the nearest placed sloka before it, before the start of the nearest placed one after it. This repeats until all are placed. The cut between two slokas is the middle of the gap between their placements. Two references are tried for every sloka: its library recording, and the learner's own most recent clean solo take of it before the recital. The run is node tools/place-by-sound.mjs; its log is below.

Contrast is the match cost against the take divided by the cost against the take played backwards: about 0.5 for the same words in the same voice, close to 1 when the reference is not in the take. Under 0.85 a placement is taken as sure; at 0.9 or more the reference was not found — the method says when it cannot place, which neither the ear nor the second opinion can say of themselves.

On the four takes whose cuts were verified by hand on 1 and 2 November (33mt, oo8n, xeca, 2dom) the sound's cuts are within half a second of the truth on every cut; on 2dom the two references agree within 0.1 s on both cuts they share.

Where it fails, and how it says so

  • A reference not in the learner's voice at the learner's pace places nothing. The library recordings of CH11-01..06 (recorded 16–17 October, 9–10 s for slokas the learner takes 14–16 s over) sit at contrast 0.92–1.02 in every CH11 take; the learner's own solo takes of CH11-04..06 place the same slokas at 0.59–0.81, in agreement with the ear and the second opinion where those agree. A sloka's reference should be a clean solo take in the learner's voice; the library recording serves when it is one (CH01, CH02, CH12, CH18 all place at 0.53–0.85).
  • A repeat or false start lies outside the reference's material and lands in the gap between two placements; the gap has to be given to a neighbour (the earlier sloka for a repeat at its end, the later one for a false start) by the pauses or the words. The cut as the gap's middle is 1 s off on CH02-05|CH02-06 of vf7e (a 2.5 s gap) for this reason.
  • An aborted take places nothing — l3fn (11 s for four slokas) places all four at 0.98–1.00 — which is the right answer, said plainly, where the ear reported two slokas.
  • Ten-sloka takes (6pdp, cpm6) leave two or three slokas at 0.86–0.89, in the doubtful band, with two references missing files; the placements are still in order and plausible, but not sure.
